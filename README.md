🐔 **Smart Chicken Coop Lighting System**

💡 **Background & Motivation**

Like all living beings, chickens require a natural daily light cycle. They thrive with 8–10 hours of darkness combined with daylight hours for optimal health. While natural sunlight is ideal, managing this in a practical chicken coop requires a reliable, automated solution.

Sudden lighting transitions can cause severe stress to the flock, leading to reduced egg production and even cannibalism. To prevent this, a smooth soft-start and soft-dimming mechanism is essential.

Commercial solutions usually present two major roadblocks for small-scale producers:

- **Internet Dependency:** Reliable Wi-Fi is rarely available inside or near a chicken coop.

- **Cost Prohibitiveness:** Smart lighting systems on the market are often too expensive to be cost-effective for small-scale setups.

This project introduces a cost-effective, standalone hardware solution built around an ESP32 microcontroller and a custom AC dimmer circuit designed specifically for two lighting bulbs.

The top-level composition of the system is illustrated below:

The system relies on four primary blocks:

- Real-Time Clock (RTC): Keeps track of accurate time independently.
- ESP32 Microcontroller: The core brain managing timing logic, zero-cross synchronization, and PWM dimming output.
- ZeroCross circuit: Provides real-time synchronization pulses to the ESP32, indicating precisely when the AC input voltage crosses zero.
- Dual AC Dimmer Circuit: Controls the sine wave intensity for two independent lighting channels (bulbs).

🛠️ **Hardware Schematic & Breakdown**

Zero-Cross Detection Circuit

<img width="1206" height="494" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/930e057e-5b9e-47b7-80ae-229bc3ae8983" />

- **Rectification:** A DF1504S-T bridge rectifier converts the input 220V AC voltage into a DC voltage.

- **Optoisolation:** The circuit uses two 120kΩ resistors in parallel to supply a small current that drives the internal LED of a 4N25 optocoupler.

- **Signal Generation:** When the AC input voltage reaches zero, the optocoupler's LED and output transistor turn OFF, pulling the ZeroCross pin HIGH (3.3V) via a pull-up resistor. During active voltage phases, the transistor turns ON, pulling the ZeroCross pin to 0V. This provides a precise hardware interrupt for the ESP32.


Real-Time Clock (RTC Block - DS1307)

<img width="732" height="534" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/0b4e038c-1075-4c09-b41f-438c8c04269c" />

- **Timekeeping & Backup:** Utilizes a standard DS1307 I2C interface circuit equipped with a crystal oscillator and a backup battery (BT1) to prevent time loss during system reboots or power outages.

- **Voltage Level Consideration:** Since the ESP32 operates at 3.3V, the pull-up resistors (R9, R10) on the SDA/SCL lines are tied to 3.3V. However, note that the DS1307 supply voltage (VCC) is 5V.


Dual Dimmer Circuit

<img width="840" height="764" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/2a8d3538-d082-4fd0-ac87-476af83ffb05" />

**Triac & Diac Control:** The dimmer uses a standard configuration combining a MOC3021XSM optoisolator and a BTA16-600B triac (Q1, Q2) to control the AC sine wave passing through the loads (Load1, Load2).

**Operation Principle:** When the control pin goes HIGH, the internal driver triggers the triac to conduct. Synchronized with the ZeroCross signal, the ESP32 precisely times the firing pulse to regulate bulb brightness smoothly.

**Spike Protection:** An RC snubber circuit (resistor R3/R8 and capacitor C1/C2) is placed in parallel with each triac to safely suppress voltage spikes generated by inductive loads like traditional lighting bulbs.

💻 **Software Overview & Communication**

The firmware is designed to be lightweight, robust, and completely independent of external internet connections.

📡 Connectivity Options

The project supports both Bluetooth Classic and Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) for initial setup and device configuration. You can easily select your preferred communication protocol using a simple #define directive in the code:

**#define USE_BLE**

This flexibility allows you to use either a smartphone application or a standard serial terminal emulator over Bluetooth to configure the system.

🕒**Setting Current Time**

To update the internal Real-Time Clock (RTC), send the time in a strict 4-digit format (hhmm):

Command Example: **1400**

Description: Sets the current time to 14:00.

**Configuring Lighting Schedules (L1 / L2)**



💡 **Setting the schedule and transition durations for a specific lamp**

Command Syntax Rule:
[Lamp][On-Time] ; [Sunrise-Duration] ; [Off-Time] ; [Sunset-Duration]

Command Example: **L12045;5;2130;10**

Parameter Breakdown:

- L1 — Target lamp identifier (L1 for Lamp 1, L2 for Lamp 2).

- 2045 — On-Time: The exact time when the bulb reaches 100% brightness.

- 5 — Sunrise Duration (minutes): The bulb begins gradually turning on 5 minutes prior (at 20:40) and reaches full brightness by 20:45.

- 2130 — Off-Time: The exact time when the bulb starts its dimming down phase.

- 10 — Sunset Duration (minutes): The bulb gradually dims down over a 10-minute period, turning completely off at 21:40.
